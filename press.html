The photo-puzzle you
send to people.

You pick a photo, Phuzzles chops it up, and whoever you send it to has to put it back together before they can see what it is. It's the group chat, turned into a game — and the one thing most photo-puzzle apps leave out: you send it.

The one-paragraph version

Phuzzles turns any photo into a puzzle you send to a friend. They can't see the picture until they solve it — your face, the dog, the view from the trip, or the news you're breaking. The link opens in a browser, so no one has to install anything to solve what you sent. There's a daily Puzzle of the Day everyone plays, streaks, and a Plus subscription that unlocks a daily pack with the story behind each picture. Free to play and to send.

Why it exists

Most photo-puzzle apps are something you play by yourself. Phuzzles was built the other way round: the puzzle is a thing you hand to another person, and the picture is the payoff for solving it. Puzzles stay private unless you make one public, and a puzzle and its photo are kept while the sender's account exists — deleting the puzzle, or the account, removes the image too.
